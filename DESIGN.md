# Design — The Best Advice I Ever Got

## The entry

> My first boss set two coffees on my desk at midnight and said, "Go home now — the work will still be here, but your kid won't be seven forever."

144 characters, one sentence, UTF-8. `main.ts` writes this literal and
nothing else to `/output/entry.txt`.

## Why this sentence

The brief weighs five things, most important first: on subject, specific and
earned, a real voice recalling a real moment, tight, and it lands.

- **A named giver and a concrete scene.** A first boss, two coffees, a desk,
  midnight. The second coffee matters: he stayed late too, so the advice
  cost him his own night.
- **Advice as spoken, not a slogan.** "Go home now" is a direct order, and
  the reason is a fact about a child's age, not a proverb.
- **A real cost.** "Your kid won't be seven forever" names what is lost by
  not listening. That is what makes it advice rather than a poster.
- **Tight.** 144 characters, one breath; shorter also wins the tie-break.

## What it is not

Not a bare aphorism — the field is flooded with "never trust…", "never
borrow…", "marry someone kind…" lines with no person and no scene, and the
brief demotes exactly that. Not addressed to the judge. Not a copy of any
field entry's wording.

## Tournament

Five drafts were judged pairwise against the three strongest field lanes
(boss-email, grandmother/waiter, ship-it-ugly) on the brief's five criteria
in order. This line was the only candidate to beat all three (3–0); the
father/bike-chain draft was second at 2–1. Full record: BUILD-REPORT.md in
the bounty directory.

## Determinism

`main.ts` writes a constant string: no timestamps, no randomness, no
network. Two local runs were replayed byte-identical before bundling.
